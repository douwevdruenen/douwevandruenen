Character Design

For as long as I can remember I've loved to draw. A few years ago, I started drawing digitally as well. Now, most of my art is done digitally with Adobe Fresco. My art mostly revolves around character design of original characters.

Worldbuilding

Many of my characters are part of a larger world that I created. This world has a rich history and a complex political landscape, which provides a lot of inspiration for my character designs. I enjoy creating characters with unique backgrounds and personalities, and I often use my art to explore different aspects of their stories.

The world I created has a fantasy setting with a diverse range of cultures, which allows for a lot of creativity in my character designs. I often draw inspiration from real-world cultures and historical periods, but I also like to add my own twists and interpretations to create something new and unique.

Character Design

My character designs are influenced by a variety of sources, including real-world mythology and history. I strive to create characters that are not only visually appealing but whose rich backstories and distinct personalities are reflected in their designs.

For example, Serena's design as seen above reflects her backstory. She was once captured by a slave trader, which is represented by the chains on the sheaths of her swords. The slave trader gave her the scar on her cheek, a tradition in parts of the world. She now hides her face behind a mask, representing the shame and trauma she wants to hide away. The design of the mask is inspired by Japanese half-masks, which adds an element of cultural depth to her design. The mask features dragon-feathers, representing the Black Feather Company, the guise of her father's crime syndicate. The scalemail around her waist also resembles feathers. Yamika, her family name meaning 'dark flower', is represented by the floral design on the guards of her swords. She has crafted her own armour, which explains its asymmetry. To balance her design, most details of her face, like the scar and her piercings, are placed on her right side, while her hair falls to her left. Lastly, the red colour scheme of her design reflects her past and her personality, representing the violence and danger she has experienced.

Inanis

Inanis is the hollow one, the Goddess of the Void. She is nothing, everything that does not exist. Her defeat at the hands of the Eternal One marked the start of time. One day, she will swallow everything that exists. This event, the Unmaking, will mark the end of time.

Her design in based on a black hole, shaped like a six-armed woman. Her size is already incomparable, yet she continues to grow with every passing second.

Tempestia

Tempestia, the Goddess of Time, is a prisoner of the Godless Pits. With the void, a contained piece of the hollow one, below and reality above, the God of Death watches over her.

The twelve chains that hold her in place represent a clock. The chains at the start of the clock have started to rust and break, while the chains at the end of the clock are still strong and intact. This represents Tempestia will one day break free.

Luna

Luna is the Goddess of the Moon and Night. Luna was corrupted by the hollow one, which is why she is the Goddess of Darkness and why she gave birth to the God of Death. It also explains her expression. Her design features elements resembling a crescent moon, as seen on her forehead, her jewellery and the shape of her hair. Her skin is dark blue and is scattered with stars, reflecting the night sky.

Older generations of Gods have six arms, at least in the religion this artwork is based on. In this artwork I wanted to show that these arms can physically interact with objects and even with each other, instead of just being symbolical.

Mortifer and Tsumi

Mortifer is the God of Death, and Tsumi, Demigoddess of Judgement, is his daughter. Mortifer carries a shepherd's crook, which is a symbol of his role as a guide for souls to the afterlife. Besides the God of Death, Mortifer is also the King of Crows, which is the reason for crows being associated with death in my world. It is also the reason for the black, feathered cloak he wears. His mask is loosely inspired by Japanese oni masks, with elements of a skull.

Tsumi wears a similar mask, though hers only covers the left side of her face. Her white hair, which is a feature present in all Demigods, is caused by the stress divine powers put on a mortal body. Tsumi wears black feathers as well, but her are not a crow's. Tsumi is the Princess of Jackdaws, who are often associated with sin, a role Tsumi once had.

Soralia

Soralia, the Fateweaver, is the Scion of Fate and Destiny. She weaves the threads of fate for all living beings, determining their destinies. She uses two arms to form the threads and two to weave them into ribbons. A crow cuts the ribbons with golden shears, representing the God of Death's influence on the end of fate.

Inspired by Ancient Greek and Roman sculptures of deities, Soralia's body is more exposed than most of my other designs. She wears no clothing, but is covered by ribbons of fate. These ribbons depict the end of the world in multiple realities, and are thus written in different scripts, including some from outside this world.

Soralia is the daughter of the Goddess of Time. After her mother's imprisonment, she was adopted by the God of Death, which is why she now wears a mask similar to his. To feel more connected to her horned father and sister, Soralia has braided her hair to resemble horns.
