In my second year of Communication and Multimedia Design (CMD), I followed an elective where I learned the fundamentals of 2D animation. I experimented with a lot of different animation styles and I eventually made a short animation. To further develop this skill in my third year, I followed an external elective of Visual Communication called 'Storytelling Fiction', where I spent half a year making an animated short film.

Scriptwriting

The script for the film was developed in the first half of the elective, and I used it as a basis for the animation process in the second half. The story is about a girl named Kaiya whose shadow comes to life, reflecting her shame and regrets. The story is a metaphor for the way we often try to hide our insecurities and mistakes, but they always find a way to catch up with us. The shadow represents the parts of ourselves that we try to suppress, but they are still a part of us and can have a powerful influence on our lives.

The story starts off with Kaiya coming home after another rough day at school. She avoids talking about her feelings with her dad, closing him off. This is shown visually by the door that separates them. This same way, there are multiple scenes that use visual metaphors to show the emotional state of Kaiya. The story then progresses to Kaiya's shadow coming to life, which is a very tense moment. The world shifts to black and white, representing her internal conflict pulling colour away. The story ends with Kaiya accepting her shadow and learning to live with it, rather than trying to hide it away.

I went through multiple iterations before I was satisfied with the story and the way it was told. I wanted to create a story that was both visually interesting and emotionally engaging, and I think the final script achieved that.

Animation process

I had decided early on I wanted to adapt my script in animation, as I had a specific look in mind that would best fit the story. After the scriptwriting process, me and my classmate, Myko Thomas, joined forces. Together, we developed the artstyle to accomodate for our differing skillsets and preferences. We wanted to create a style that was simple and minimalistic, but still had a lot of character and emotion. We went through multiple iterations before we were satisfied with the final look.

With the visual style established, we could finally start animating. We knew there wasn't enough time to finish the entire story, so we decided to work on the first couple scenes as a preview. I started off by creating the animatic, which is a rough version of the animation that helps to establish the timing and pacing of the story.

After the animatic was finished, we started working on the final animation.

Editing and sound design

In between animating shots for the film, I had a recording session with my friend Denise Joosten, who became the voice of Kaiya and her shadow. For the shadow, I had her record lines in a whispering voice. These lines voice Kaiya's inner doubts and regrets. I then layered these recordings on top of each other, making them more and more overwhelming as the shadow starts to emerge,

In the final stage, I started the editing process. I combined our hand-drawn animations with the voice acting, some sounds Myko had recorded and some open-source sound effects to enhance the story and the tension. After a lot of polishing, the final animation was completed. I'm especially proud of the scene where the shadow first emerges. Myko's animation combined with my editing and sound design forms a truly tense moment.
